# S7 audit: statistical methodology of the S6 feasibility gate (design-level review)

The gate (`quantlab/feasibility.py`, S6) and its thresholds are **unchanged** (S6 acceptance test). Everything below is a seeded Monte Carlo on **synthetic** returns (`quantlab/gate_audit.py`, results `s7_gate_audit_results.json`, pinned by `tests/test_gate_audit.py`, re-run byte-identical). Simulations describe the behaviour of the gate's formulas; they are not market evidence. Measured repository facts used: S3 regime structure (5 bull, 7 sideways, 2 bear episodes in 2021-2025), S4 point-in-time depth.

| # | Question | Verdict | Key evidence (synthetic) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does the gate favour high market exposure? | **Yes, by construction; the arithmetic is right, the choice of statistic is not neutral** | MDE of the ACTIVE-day Sharpe falls with the invested-day share: 10 % -> 3.92, 20 % -> 2.76, 40 % -> 1.95, 60 % -> 1.59, 80 % -> 1.38, 90 % -> 1.30, 100 % -> 1.23. A t-test on the calendar series and on the active days have identical power (0.25 at 20 % invested, 0.69 at 90 % for a true active Sharpe of 1), so the gate is internally coherent: it measures whether an ACTIVE-day Sharpe can be detected, and fewer active days means less power. |
| 2 | Do episode counts represent independent observations? | **No** | Overlapping holding periods make the mean episode return far less precise than the gate's Kish factor 1 + (m-1)rho assumes. True design effect vs gate formula: 1-day holds 1.43 vs 1.22; 5-day 2.51 vs 1.20; 20-day **7.77 vs 1.16**. At 20-day holds the effective number of episodes is 37, the gate would credit 250. |
| 3 | Does the effective-sample adjustment cover cross-sectional correlation, overlap and regimes? | **Only within-day correlation; not overlap, not regimes** | With a shared slow regime mean the true design effect rises again (1-day 2.07, 5-day 3.54, 20-day 9.29 against ~1.2). Independent regime episodes measured in this repository: 2 bear episodes (S3); the gate has no regime criterion. |
| 4 | Is the MDE valid for autocorrelation, non-normality and limited history? | **Adequate for daily returns; duration is the binding limit** | Size of a one-sided 5 % Sharpe test: 0.048-0.088 (naive) and 0.047-0.075 (Newey-West) across iid normal, Student-t(3), AR(0.15), volatility clustering, n = 500 and 1 642; power at the stated MDE 0.70-0.83 (target 0.8). Fat tails and mild autocorrelation matter little at daily frequency. The MDE is 2.13 for 500 days and 1.17 for 1 642 days: the 4.5-year window is the limit, not the distribution. |
| 5 | Are low-frequency defensive strategies rejected for statistically justified reasons? | **Not when the claim is timing or defence; justified when the claim is an absolute active-day Sharpe** | A circular-block random-timing null detects a given timing skill equally well at 20 % and 90 % invested: skill kappa 0.02 -> 0.575 vs 0.65, kappa 0.05 -> 1.00 vs 1.00; size 0.09 / 0.075 at kappa 0 (80 repetitions). So A7 (absolute Sharpe on active days) rejects low-exposure designs for reasons that do not apply to a paired or timing-based claim. Caveat: the skill levels are chosen by me; realistic skill is unknown and probably smaller. |
| 6 | Are declared exposure assumptions realistic after the frozen risk layer? | **No for crypto-like volatility; the gate cannot check it** | A fully invested equal-weight book run through the frozen S2 layer (synthetic, daily, 8 assets, factor correlation 0.6): mean invested-day share **0.65 / 0.37 / 0.32** at portfolio volatility ~31 % / 54 % / 77 %; **58-68 % of paths end in SHUTDOWN**. Small constant sleeves do not escape either (15-25 % of the wallet: 27-57 % of paths shut down, almost all through the 3-flat-cycle cap, not the 18 % floor; the synthetic basket has a harsh geometric drift, so read it as relative evidence). Re-running the UNCHANGED gate arithmetic on the B2 draft with these shares: MDE p95 **1.74 / 2.42 / 2.69 > 1.5 -> FAIL** (it passes only at the declared 0.9-1.0). |
| 7 | Does benchmark selection or ex-post matching mislead? | **No flattering bias found; comparators are demanding but not tradable** | Under a true null (random exposure, no skill): rejection rate of the paired bootstrap against the plain benchmark 0.02 and against the best of 5 correlated baskets 0.01 (conservative); mean Sharpe difference -0.09; only 1.5 % of no-skill paths meet the 20 % drawdown limit and 0.5 % also beat the ex-post cash-mix. Exposure-matched comparators remove everything except timing by construction; volatility-matched ones keep the benchmark's Sharpe (scale invariance). Ex-post comparators (volatility-matched, cash-mix) use whole-path information and must stay labelled as non-tradable. |

## Conclusions
1. The gate is a **necessary** screen and is statistically coherent for what it states, but it (a) overstates effective sample size when positions overlap or regimes dominate, (b) has no way to check declared exposure against the risk layer, (c) uses a statistic that structurally penalises defensive designs, and (d) cannot test whether the plausible effect exceeds the MDE.
2. With the frozen risk layer and a single 4.5-year cycle, the detectable effect is about an active/relative Sharpe of 1.2 or more; plausible crypto anomaly sizes are lower, so most designs are unvalidatable on this dataset regardless of the gate.
3. The existing gate stays as it is. Improvements are proposed, not adopted, in `proposals/feasibility_methodology_v2_proposal.json` (separately versioned, with adoption criteria).
